## The question

"Agriculture is the backbone of Ghana's economy" is one of the most repeated lines about the
country. This episode puts it to the test with three numbers, all from 2025: **jobs**,
**output** and **bank loans**.

## The data

- **Jobs:** the Ghana Statistical Service's Quarterly Labour Force Survey, employment by
  sector, Q3 2025.
- **Output and growth:** the Ghana Statistical Service's 2025 annual GDP release (provisional),
  published in March 2026.
- **Bank loans:** the Bank of Ghana's January 2026 Monetary Policy Report. Its Table A.5 gives
  banks' outstanding credit by sector in cedis for December 2024 and December 2025.

## The approach

- **One year, three shares.** Each share is shown as a number "in every hundred", so the
  comparison is about proportions, not totals.
- **Loans outstanding, not new lending.** The Bank of Ghana also publishes sector shares of
  *new* lending, which can be negative and don't add up to 100%. The story uses only the stock
  of loans outstanding.
- **One denominator.** Agriculture is 4.3% of banks' *total* credit. Press reports quoting 4.5%
  use *private-sector* credit instead: the same loans, measured against a smaller total.
- **The twist, from the Bank of Ghana's own table.**
  - Total bank loans: GH¢95.5bn at December 2024, rising to GH¢111.0bn at December 2025 (+16.2%).
  - Agriculture: GH¢4.50bn, rising to GH¢4.81bn (+6.9%).
  - So agriculture's loans grew, but more slowly than the total, and its share fell from 4.7%
    to 4.3%.

## The discovery

**By jobs, agriculture is a backbone.** About **37 in every 100** working Ghanaians work in
agriculture, around five million people.

**By output, it still is.** About **23 in every 100** cedis of economic output in 2025 came from
agriculture.

**By bank loans, it is not.** At the end of 2025, just over **4 in every 100** cedis that Ghana's
banks had out on loan were in agriculture.

That isn't because agriculture is shrinking. In 2025 it grew **6.8%**, faster than the
economy's **6.0%**. Bank loans to agriculture went up too, but bank loans overall went up faster,
so agriculture's share fell from **4.7% to 4.3%**.

## Limitations

- **Bank loans are only part of farm finance.** The figures cover deposit money banks.
  Microfinance, rural and community banks, input credit from buyers and outgrower schemes are
  outside the measure.
- **Which GDP share.** The 22.8% is agriculture's share of GDP at current prices (nominal), from
  the GSS annual GDP tables. Measured at constant 2013 prices it would be about 22%.
- **Provisional figures.** The 2025 GDP figures are provisional and may be revised.
- **No causes are claimed.** The video shows what the data say. It doesn't explain why banks
  lend less to agriculture (risk, collateral, interest rates and so on).
