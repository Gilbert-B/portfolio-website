## The question

Two things are said about Accra at the same time: it is **booming**, and the city itself is
**emptying out**. Both can be backed by census numbers. Can both be true — and what exactly
is meant by "Accra"?

## The data

Ghana Statistical Service census results for **2010 and 2021** at regional and district
level, with regional totals back to 1970 for the long-run view. Maps use geoBoundaries
administrative boundaries (CC BY 4.0).

## The approach

- **Region first.** Compare Greater Accra and Ashanti at every census from 1970 to 2021,
  with 1970 figures reconstructed for today's regional boundaries.
- **Then the city.** The old Accra Metropolitan area has since been split into several
  districts. To compare like with like, the 2010 metropolitan total was revised to exclude
  La Dade-Kotopon (which separated in 2012), and the 2021 populations of the ten related
  successor districts were added together.
- **Look at where growth went** by mapping district-level change, including fast-growing
  districts on the edge of the region such as Kpone Katamanso.
- **Verify the regional totals** against GSS figures and reproduce the district arithmetic.

## The discovery

- **Greater Accra is growing.** In 2021 it counted **5,455,692** residents, overtaking
  Ashanti (5,440,463) by **15,229** — the first census in which Ashanti was not the larger
  region. In 2010, Ashanti had been ahead by 770,326.
- **The old city's reported total fell.** The revised 2010 Accra Metropolitan figure is
  **1,665,086**; the ten related districts in 2021 total **1,281,570** — a gap of
  **383,516**.
- Meanwhile, districts on the outskirts grew fast: **Kpone Katamanso rose from 109,864 to
  388,295** (2010 figure for today's boundary).

The region and the old city are moving in different directions — which is why "Accra is
growing" and "Accra is shrinking" can both be heard. It depends on which Accra.

## Limitations

- **Boundaries changed between the two censuses.** An official crosswalk confirming that the
  2010 metropolitan footprint and the combined 2021 districts cover exactly the same land has
  not been found. The 383,516 gap is therefore **not presented as a confirmed population
  loss** from an identical area.
- The census records **where people were counted on census night** — not where they work,
  or why anyone moved.
- These numbers do not show that people left the old city, or that they moved to Kpone
  Katamanso or anywhere else. Migration would need different data.
