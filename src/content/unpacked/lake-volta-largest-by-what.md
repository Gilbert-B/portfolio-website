## The question

Ghanaians are often told that **Lake Volta is the largest man-made lake in the world**.
The claim is true, but only for one measure. What does "largest" mean for a lake: the area it
covers, or the water it holds?

## The data

Published reference figures for the two biggest contenders:

- **Lake Volta in Ghana**, from the Volta River Authority.
- **Lake Kariba on the Zambia–Zimbabwe border**, from the Zambezi River Authority.

Mean depths are cross-checked against the ILEC World Lake Database.
Lake outlines come from Natural Earth and OpenStreetMap.

## The approach

- **Two measures, kept separate:** surface area (km²) and storage capacity at full supply
  level (km³).
- **A fair map comparison:** both lakes are drawn on one **equal-area projection** at the same
  scale, so the size you see on screen is proportional to the real area.
- **Simple arithmetic that anyone can check:**
  - 181 − 148 = 33 km³;
  - 148 trillion litres ÷ 5,000 litres = 29.6 billion tanks.
- **Depth shown carefully:** the depth visuals use published mean depths and are labelled as
  illustrations, not surveyed lake profiles.

## The discovery

**By surface area, Lake Volta is the largest.** It covers about **8,500 km²**, against about
**5,600 km²** for Lake Kariba.

**By storage capacity, Lake Kariba is the larger.** At full capacity it holds about
**181 km³**, against Lake Volta's **148 km³**. That is more than 30 km³ of extra water,
despite the smaller area.

The reason is depth. Lake Volta spreads wide and shallow. Lake Kariba fills a deeper valley:
on average it is about **one and a half times as deep** (29.2 m against 18.8 m).

Both lakes are enormous, in different ways. So when you hear "the world's largest man-made
lake", the useful question is: **largest by what?**

## Limitations

- **Capacities are full-supply figures.** The actual volume of water in either lake rises and
  falls with rainfall, inflow and dam operations.
- **Published mean depths and capacities don't fully agree.** Multiplying area by mean depth
  doesn't exactly reproduce either lake's capacity, probably because the sources use different
  water levels or survey dates. The video therefore never presents area × depth as a
  calculation of volume.
- **The map outlines are close but not exact.** The drawn outlines measure 94–96% of the
  published areas, so they illustrate shape and scale; they are not survey maps.
- **The tank comparison is an analogy.** The tank is a generic 5,000-litre drawing and
  doesn't represent any brand.
